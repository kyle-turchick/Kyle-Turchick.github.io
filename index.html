Research program

Three research lines, one ecological question: how does variation become biodiversity?

Heterogeneity-diversity theory

I test how abiotic environmental variation influences plant and bird richness across local to continental scales, including whether biodiversity responses are linear, saturating, or unimodal.

Renewable-energy ecology

I evaluate how utility-scale solar development alters desert vegetation, soil-surface conditions, biological soil crust, woody debris, burrows, plant-community structure, and geodiversity.

Disturbance and recovery

I study how landslides, wildfire, windstorms, mechanical vegetation treatments, and solar-facility construction reorganize biomass, carbon, vegetation structure, and biodiversity.

Featured projects

Field systems as living instruments.

The work spans public-land monitoring networks, desert solar landscapes, and disturbance-carbon gradients. Each project is designed to move from field evidence to applied ecological inference.

01

Yellow Pine Solar Facility ecological monitoring

Mojave Desert, Nevada · Bureau of Land Management Las Vegas Field Office · 2022-2024

Permanent transects compare utility-scale solar-facility disturbance, undisturbed controls, and a debris-flow/landslide reference system. The dataset tracks perennial and annual vegetation, species richness, biological soil crust, woody debris, burrows, surface rock cover, landforms, slope shape, and photovoltaic-panel microsites.

02

Western U.S. geodiversity-biodiversity synthesis

BLM monitoring networks · eBird/species-richness data · ecoregion frameworks

This work integrates federal vegetation and soil monitoring data, bird richness estimates, geodiversity metrics, and ecoregional frameworks to evaluate scale-dependent plant and bird biodiversity patterns across public lands.

03

Southeast Alaska disturbance-carbon gradients

Temperate rainforest systems · University of Colorado Denver · NSF-supported research

Field-collected vegetation and soil data evaluate how landslide and windstorm disturbance gradients influence biomass, soil conditions, disturbance history, and carbon stocks in high-density carbon temperate rainforest ecosystems.
